Oreneta Privacy Policy

Last updated: 13 October 2026

The short version

Oreneta is a desktop application. It has no server of its own, no accounts of its own, no analytics, no telemetry and no crash reporting. Your mail, calendars, contacts, notes and credentials stay on your computer and travel only to the servers of the accounts you add, and to the few services listed below. Each of those is named here with what it receives, and the tables say which ones happen on their own and which only when you turn them on or ask for them.

What Oreneta stores, and where

  • Mail, calendars, contacts, tasks, notes and chat are kept in a local database on your computer, so the app works fast and offline.
  • Credentials (account passwords, OAuth tokens, app passwords) are kept in your operating system's keychain. On Linux without a keychain service they are kept in a local encrypted file whose key is stored beside it, which protects them less than a keychain. A proxy password is kept in the local database, not in the keychain.
  • Profile backups, made by hand or on a schedule, are encrypted with a password you choose and saved where you choose. They never include your account passwords or tokens; they do include your proxy settings, with a proxy password if you set one.
  • The settings backup file (Settings, Backup, Export) is encrypted only if you give it a password. If you tick Include account passwords, it also carries your account passwords and tokens, and a password is then required.
  • Removing an account deletes its local data and its stored credentials, except what you wrote and had not sent: unsent local drafts, scheduled sends and the record of sends whose outcome was uncertain are kept, and none of them is sent once the account is gone. Deleting a profile can delete all of that profile's data.

Downloads from github.com (updates, translation languages, the changelog) are served from GitHub's download servers (*.githubusercontent.com).

Meeting links (Meet, Zoom, Teams and others) are only recognised in event text and opened in your browser; Oreneta does not contact those services itself. A Google Meet link is created through Google Calendar only when you tick it.

If you set a proxy, connections for your accounts, feeds (and the pictures and icons saved from them during a sync), file links, chat files, translation downloads, the tracker list, the changelog and the assistant go through it, and so do the sign-in exchange and token refreshes with Google or Microsoft, finding server settings, your account picture, and the update check and download.

An account can also have its own proxy, or connect directly. IMAP, SMTP, POP3, server rules, Microsoft Graph, Exchange (EWS), Google Calendar, Google contacts, and the CalDAV calendars and calendar subscriptions you keep under an account follow that choice, and so does the account's picture. CardDAV address books added from Settings and CalDAV task lists are not kept under an account, so they use the app's proxy, or connect directly if the app has none.

If the proxy cannot be reached, these connections fail instead of going around it. The same holds for a proxy you chose but left without a server or port, for the app or for one account, and for a proxy setting that could not be read: every connection that would use it is refused until it is completed or can be read again, and none goes directly. Connections already open when the proxy changes keep going until they reconnect. If your account picture could not be downloaded, Oreneta keeps its address and your web view loads it from there each time, without the proxy.

Finding server settings skips the DNS lookup while a proxy is set, because DNS cannot go through it. Pictures shown in mail, pictures and videos shown in a feed item that are not saved on your computer, correspondents' pictures, and the sign-in page that opens in your browser do not use the app's proxy: your web view and browser connect on their own.

Your operating system's web view (for example Microsoft Edge WebView2 on Windows) may make connections of its own, under its vendor's policy.

Google user data

When you sign in with Google, Oreneta requests full access to Gmail (used to read, send, move, label and delete your mail as you ask), your basic profile (to label the account with your name, address and picture), full access to Google Calendar (used to show your calendars and to create, edit and delete the events you ask it to) and your contacts, read only (to suggest addresses).

Oreneta's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. In particular:

  • Google user data is used only to provide the mail, calendar and contact features you see, on your own computer.
  • It is never sold, never used for advertising, never used to train models, and no human other than you reads it.
  • It is not transferred to anyone except as a feature you use requires, each one listed above under Connections: the assistant with a remote provider (only what you reviewed and confirmed, for that request; the assistant is off until you set it up), pictures in a message you choose to show, read receipts you send, and, if you turn on real pictures for correspondents, a hash of an address to Gravatar and its domain to DuckDuckGo.

Revoking access

Remove an account in Oreneta's settings and its local data and stored credentials are deleted, except unsent drafts and scheduled sends, which are kept as described above. You can also revoke Oreneta's access to your Google account at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions, and to your Microsoft account at account.live.com/consent/Manage (work and school accounts: myapps.microsoft.com).
